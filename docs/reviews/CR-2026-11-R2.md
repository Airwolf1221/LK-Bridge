# CR-2026-11-R2: the second independent review, at write scope

DRAFT-FIRST-LINE

An independent review of everything merged on `main` since the first review (`08feec9`, 11 November 2026) up to
`013c988` (17 November 2026, F3.4, #65): 33 pull requests, about 78,000 lines of source and 67,000 of tests, and ADRs
0037 to 0055. The reason for it: on 17 November the owner moved Claude and ChatGPT to scope `write` on Arkona. Ordinary
changes now go straight through and the owner reviews them afterwards on the Review page; canon and lifecycle changes
are still proposals, and the "always propose" rails turn deletes, bulk changes over 10 pages, and asset deletes and moves
into proposals. Every part of that was built by sessions that wrote their own tests.

**How it was done.** As CR-2026-11 (its method and format). The reviewer did not write the code and treated every
"verified", "tested" and "covered" in the reports, the changelog and the ADRs as a claim to check. The write-scope engine
(the gate, the rails, the guards' order, the Review queue), the restore planner, the chronology's orphan delete, the
dashboard's routes and re-authentication, Last Major Revision's hook and the governor's learned limits were read in full
by the lead reviewer. Four separate reviewers, each in an isolated copy of the repository and each given the code and the
ADRs rather than the lead's conclusions, tried to break write scope, Revert, the image paths and the guards through the
setting packs (section 4 lists every attempt, successful or not). Every finding below was reproduced by a test that
asserts the safe behaviour and **fails on the reviewed code**; each fix makes it pass. **Nothing was run against a live
system**: every test runs against the mock LegendKeeper in this repository; the owner's LegendKeeper bridge connector,
the live dashboard and LegendKeeper were not called; no sandbox, soak, spike, rehearsal or round-trip script was run. The
new sandbox checks of section 6 are written and dry-run against the mock only; the owner runs them (section 8).

**Severity**, as CR-2026-11 defines it: critical — could lose or corrupt data, or pass canon or lifecycle approval; high
— a wrong result or a misleading state; medium — cost or robustness; low — clarity.
