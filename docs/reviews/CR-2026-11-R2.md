# CR-2026-11-R2: the second independent review, at write scope

**Critical: on the reviewed code an agent at scope `write` could damage Arkona.** It could bring deleted Authoritative
pages back without canonical approval (R2-10), and a setting pack's creation tools could make a decoy lifecycle index,
directly where the owner had set the tool to follow the scope (R2-11). Both are fixed in this branch, with every other
critical and high finding; keep Claude and ChatGPT at scope `propose` on Arkona until it is deployed.

An independent review of everything merged on `main` since the first review (`08feec9`, 11 November 2026) up to
`013c988` (17 November 2026, F3.4, #65): 33 pull requests, about 78,000 lines of source and 67,000 of tests, and ADRs
0037 to 0055. The reason for it: on 17 November the owner moved Claude and ChatGPT to scope `write` on Arkona. Ordinary
changes now go straight through and the owner reviews them afterwards on the Review page; canon and lifecycle changes
are still proposals, and the "always propose" rails turn deletes, bulk changes over 10 pages, and asset deletes and
moves into proposals. Every part of that was built by sessions that wrote their own tests. F3.5 (#66, the block-id
sweep, ADR 0056) reached `main` while this review ran: it is merged into this branch and its tests pass with every fix,
but it was not reviewed (section 7). This review's owner decisions are therefore ADR 0057.

**How it was done.** As CR-2026-11 (its method and format). The reviewer did not write the code and treated every
"verified", "tested" and "covered" in the reports, the changelog and the ADRs as a claim to check. The write-scope
engine (the gate, the rails, the guards' order, the Review queue), the restore planner, the chronology's orphan delete,
the dashboard's routes and re-authentication, Last Major Revision's hook and the governor's learned limits were read in
full by the lead reviewer. Four separate reviewers, each in an isolated copy of the repository and each given the code
and the ADRs rather than the lead's conclusions, tried to break write scope, Revert, the image paths and the guards
through the setting packs (section 4 lists every attempt, successful or not). Every finding below was reproduced by a
test that asserts the safe behaviour and **fails on the reviewed code**; each fix makes it pass. **Nothing was run
against a live system**: every test runs against the mock LegendKeeper in this repository; the owner's LegendKeeper
bridge connector, the live dashboard and LegendKeeper were not called; no sandbox, soak, spike, rehearsal or round-trip
script was run. The new sandbox checks of section 6 are written and dry-run against the mock only; the owner runs them
(section 8).

**Severity**, as CR-2026-11 defines it: critical — could lose or corrupt data, or pass canon or lifecycle approval; high
— a wrong result or a misleading state; medium — cost or robustness; low — clarity.

### R2-01 (High): template copies with their children counted as one page

- **Found by:** the write-scope pass (WS-B1, WS-B2). The bulk rail counted `createResourceFromTemplate` and
  `saveResourceAsTemplate` as one page each (`CREATES_ONE`, src/policy/rails.ts), ignoring `includeChildren`, though one
  call copies up to 100 pages: a template with 15 pages under it made 16 pages directly.
- **Fix:** the bulk rail counts a template copy, or a page saved as a template, with `includeChildren` as one page plus
  every page under its source (the template, or the page saved); when those pages are not known (templates are never in
  the repository tree) it fails closed, as an unknown cascade does (src/policy/rails.ts `COPIES`, `pagesTouched`). At
  scope `write` such a copy is a proposal unless the bridge can count it.
- **Test:** `test/review/r2-writescope/bulk.test.ts` (B1, B2).

### R2-02 (High): three destructive operations passed the deletes rail

- **Found by:** the write-scope pass (WS-A1 to WS-A4, WS-R2). `RAIL_DELETES` named `deleteBoardShapes`, `deleteCalendar`
  and `deleteProperty`, but not `replaceBoardContent` (which deletes every shape, binding and asset it is not sent),
  `updateCalendar` (a PUT that replaces the whole calendar, so every date on every timeline is shown by the new one) or
  `deleteBlockProperty` (an infobox property and its rows). Revert puts none of them back; only the journal's pre-image
  does, by hand.
- **Fix:** `replaceBoardContent` ("board content replaced"), `updateCalendar` ("calendar replaced") and
  `deleteBlockProperty` ("infobox property") are deletes for the rail (`RAIL_DELETES`). One existing test changed:
  `test/policy/rails.test.ts` moves `deleteBlockProperty` from "never a delete" into the deletes cases, and adds the two
  whole replaces and the copies with their children.
- **Test:** `test/review/r2-writescope/deletes.test.ts` (A1 to A4), `routes.test.ts` (R2, through `lk_op_*`).

### R2-03 (Medium, open): a restore counted as one page

- **Found by:** the write-scope pass (WS-B3, WS-B6). LegendKeeper restores every page deleted by the same delete; the
  bulk rail counted the restored page only.
- **Open:** an owner decision (ADR 0057 decision 9; counting the pages from the restore set the canon guard now reads is
  recommended). Since R2-10, a restore that brings back an Authoritative page is a canonical proposal whatever its size.
  The tests are kept as `it.fails`.
- **Test:** `bulk.test.ts` (B3, B6).

### R2-04 (Medium): a cascade's pages counted from a stale tree

- **Found by:** the write-scope pass (WS-B7, with the deletes rail switched off). The bulk rail took a cascade's size
  from the repository tree; a page the tree knows, with children it has not seen, counted as having none, not as
  unknown, so ADR 0055 decision C2's "fails closed" did not hold.
- **Fix:** when the tree's counts would let a cascade delete or a copy with its children run directly, the gate counts
  the pages again as LegendKeeper holds them (the canon guard's `listSubtree`, at most about N + 1 reads at P1), and the
  larger count wins; a listing that fails, fails closed (`LivePolicyGate.rails`, now async; `liveDescendants` in
  src/runtime.ts). A request already proposed costs no reads.
- **Test:** `bulk.test.ts` (B7).

### R2-05 (High): an approved create accepted a page an agent had squatted under its id

- **Found by:** the write-scope pass (WS-S1). The bridge mints a page's id when a proposal is captured, and tells the
  agent. A `DUPLICATE_ID` on the first send of `createManyResources` was treated as "may have applied", and recovery
  accepted the step because a page with the id existed: an agent that created its own page under the minted id before
  the owner approved had the approved job report `executed`, with the owner's facts then written on the agent's page.
- **Fix:** a `DUPLICATE_ID` on the first send of a `createManyResources` step is a refusal (LegendKeeper checks every
  item before it creates any: sandbox W14): the step fails with the 409; only a step sent before, or one continuing a
  partial batch, is recovered (`sentBefore`). And a page found under a client id counts as the step's only when it is
  the page planned, by its name (trimmed) and its parent (`templates` for a saved template); otherwise the step is
  uncertain, saying what differs (`notAsPlanned`, src/jobs/step-exec.ts).
- **Test:** `test/review/r2-writescope/shared-id.test.ts`.

### R2-06 (Medium): the Review page showed the agent's own title as the summary

- **Found by:** the write-scope pass (WS-E1). For a low-level job the summary fell back to the job's title, which an
  agent chooses for `lk_api_batch`: a batch that wiped a board was listed as "write_page append on “Ashgate”: fixed a
  typo". A proposal of the same batch is summarised from its operations.
- **Fix:** a low-level job (`lk_api_*`, `lk_op_*`) is summarised as its proposal would be, from its operations
  (`proposalSummary`); the agent's own title is shown apart, as "agent's title: …" (`agentTitle`). The test's set-up
  switches the deletes rail off first (it relied on R2-02's gap to run directly); its assertions are unchanged.
- **Test:** `test/review/r2-writescope/review-queue.test.ts`.

### R2-07 (Medium): placing an old-style staged image exempted any asset move

- **Found by:** the write-scope pass (WS-F1). Placing an image staged the old way (in LegendKeeper's staging folder)
  marks the request a staged placement, which the asset rail does not count (ADR 0055 C4); `place()` never checked that
  the asset was still in the staging folder, so an asset the owner had since filed in a curated folder was moved
  directly, and a revert of that job would have proposed deleting the owner's own asset.
- **Fix:** placing an old-style staged asset reads it live first (one `getAsset`): the request is a staged placement
  only while the asset is in the staging folder; otherwise, or when it cannot be read, the move is an ordinary one and
  the asset rail decides (a proposal by default) (`assetInStagingFolder`, src/images/service.ts).
- **Test:** `test/review/r2-writescope/staged-asset.test.ts`.

### R2-08 (Medium): a chronology link an agent chose at scope `write` was never on the Review page

- **Found by:** the lead reviewer, checking which direct jobs the Review queue records. `sync_timeline_event` with
  `event_id` on an event that already agrees records "this row is that event" (ADR 0048 F2.1): no LegendKeeper write,
  but it decides what the check pairs, repairs and offers to delete from then on, which is why it is a proposal at scope
  `propose`. At scope `write` it ran directly, and its job holds only the bridge's own steps (kind `read`), which the
  Review queue never records: the owner never saw it, and Revert never offered to undo it.
- **Fix:** a chosen link is a proposal at every scope (`forceProposal`), as the dashboard's "Pair with this row" already
  was; the tool's description, the bridge guide and docs/chronology.md say so. The alternative (direct but listed, with
  Revert removing the link) is ADR 0057 decision 3. Three existing tests that linked directly at scope `write` now
  approve the proposal first (`test/chronology/sync.test.ts`, `ties.test.ts` twice).
- **Test:** `test/chronology/r2-link-review.test.ts`.

### R2-09 (Low): "Mark all reviewed" compared the count, not the set

- **Found by:** the lead reviewer, checking ADR 0055 decision A3's claim ("refused … when the set changed in the
  meantime") against `ReviewService.markAll`: only the count was compared. One item marked reviewed in another tab and a
  new direct write arriving left the count unchanged, and the new item, which the owner never saw, was marked reviewed.
- **Fix:** the list answers a fingerprint of the exact set (`markAll.fingerprint`, a hash of the ids); the dashboard
  sends it back with the count, and a different set is refused (`REVIEW_CHANGED`).
- **Test:** `test/admin/r2-mark-all.test.ts`.

### R2-10 (Critical): a cascade restore brought back Authoritative pages without canonical approval

- **Found by:** the write-scope pass (WS-C1). Restoring a deleted Authoritative page alone is a canonical proposal, but
  the canon guard judged only the restored page (`restoredPage`, src/facts/canonical.ts) and the lifecycle guard's
  `restoreChange` only the top page, while LegendKeeper restores every page deleted by the same delete. An agent at
  scope `write` restored `[INDEX] Locations` (deleted in the app with the pages under it) and Old Harbour came back with
  Status Authoritative and CANON, directly.
- **Fix:** a restore is judged on every page it may bring back. `readRestoreSet` (src/lifecycle/deleted.ts) lists them
  from the bridge's journal of its own delete (the `delete_resource` subtree), the newest snapshots holding the page
  (walked back to a full snapshot, or to the change snapshot of a bridge delete) and the tree while it still holds the
  page; each page's facts come from its newest snapshot; at most 100 pages, and more, or no source, is "not known". The
  canon guard judges each as a new page at entry (`restoredWith`) and now inside the lock too (`restoreInLock`: it had
  no restore check there); a set not known, or a page with no facts in any snapshot, is a canonical proposal; a restore
  in a request that also moves, creates or restores counts as unchecked. The lifecycle guard judges each page as a move
  from its area to where it lands (`restoreChanges`). Pages the bridge's own delete took are not judged (restoring them
  needs no approval, as before). `restore_resource`'s description no longer says no canonical approval is needed. One
  sandbox check changed: round 1's A10 now restores through the governor (section 6).
- **Test:** `test/review/r2-writescope/restore-canon.test.ts` (C1; the control, a canon page restored alone, still a
  canonical proposal).

### R2-11 (Critical): a setting pack could make a lifecycle-index decoy

- **Found by:** the packs pass (PK-01a, PK-01b, PK-02). Every index a pack package makes is declared in the job's
  lifecycle marker with `to: null`, and the lifecycle guard's `declares()` read that as declaring every change on the
  page, including the creation of a page named as a lifecycle index; inside the lock a template copy carrying a name was
  not judged. `arkona_create_character` named "Working" made a second `[INDEX] Working` inside the real one (the
  lifecycle tools then stop: the index is "ambiguous"); `arkona_create_owner` with racial group "Archived" made
  `[INDEX] Archived`. With the default switches the package is an ordinary proposal, so the owner's ordinary approval
  made it; on "follow the scope" it was direct. The same create through `lk_api_write` is refused (CR-15).
- **Fix:** no lifecycle marker declares a lifecycle index change (a page given a lifecycle index's name, or the index
  itself), whatever tool sets it (`declares()`, src/lifecycle/guard.ts), and every template copy is judged inside the
  lock by its own name or its template's. A package that would make `[INDEX] Working` is refused at capture
  (`POLICY_DENIED`), as the same create through `lk_api_write` is. One reviewer test's set-up changed: PK-02 now accepts
  that refusal or an approved ordinary proposal, as PK-01a does; its safety assertion (no second `[INDEX] Archived`) is
  unchanged.
- **Test:** `test/review/r2-packs/index-imitation.test.ts`, `owners.test.ts` (PK-02).

### R2-12 (High): an Authoritative page renamed out of `[BSRC]` directly

- **Found by:** the write-scope pass (WS-C2b). `prefixChange` treated only `[BWRK]` and `[BARC]` as lifecycle prefixes;
  a rename into `[BSRC]` is canonical (ADR 0036 decision 1) but a rename out of it was not: `[BSRC] Old Harbour` became
  `Old Harbour` with Status Authoritative still, and the lifecycle tools later refuse a page whose name and Status
  disagree.
- **Fix:** a rename that drops `[BSRC]` (read loosely, as CR-14's `looseKind` does) is a canonical change, at entry and
  in the lock (`canonRename`); only a canonical marker declares a canon change.
- **Test:** `test/review/r2-writescope/canon-lifecycle.test.ts` (C2b).

### R2-13 (High): status tags arrived through template copies

- **Found by:** the write-scope pass (WS-D1a to WS-D1c). The lifecycle guard judged only tags in a create's body, on the
  premise that `createResourceFromTemplate` takes none; per the spec a copy takes the template's fields, tags included,
  and a template saved from a page carries that page's tags. A copy of a CANON-tagged template, and an archived set's
  index saved and copied outside the archive (tag Archived), made status-tagged pages directly, against ADR 0036
  decision 7. Spec-modelled (the reviewer's mock copies a template's tags); sandbox check W13 tests it on the real API.
- **Fix:** at entry a copy takes its template's tags (one cached `getResource`), a template saved earlier in the same
  request carries its page's tags, and pages copied with `includeChildren` are judged on their own tags (`listSubtree`
  now returns them); inside the lock a copy an agent asks for reads the template and judges its name, tags and Status. A
  template whose tags cannot be read refuses the copy. The bridge's own marked jobs (the creation tools, which retag
  their copies themselves) are exempt; an agent's request carries no marker. Cost: on the real API two reads more per
  template copy; the sandbox's round 1 dry run reads 531 → 589 (its cap 540 → 600; with R2-14).
- **Test:** `canon-lifecycle.test.ts` (D1a, D1b, D1c).

### R2-14 (High): a Working page made inside canon when the tree had not seen a move

- **Found by:** the packs pass (PK-09). A create's area came from the repository tree at entry, the prefix rule judged
  `[BWRK]` and not where the page lands, and inside the lock a named template copy was not judged: after the owner moved
  a Working set's index into canon in the app, `arkona_add_character_art` (direct by default) made
  `[BWRK] Tamsin Reed - Character Art` under the now-canon index.
- **Fix:** inside the lock, `createResource`, `createManyResources` and template copies are judged on where the page
  lands now (`landingLive`): the parent is read live when it matters (the page belongs in an area by its prefix, status
  tag or marker, or the tree places the parent in one); a page that belongs in Working or Archived but lands elsewhere
  is covered by no marker. One existing test changed: `test/packs/costs.test.ts`, the Art job's reads 28 → 29 (the live
  read of the character's index).
- **Test:** `test/review/r2-packs/working-into-canon.test.ts`.

### R2-15 (Medium): status-tag look-alikes outside the fold

- **Found by:** the packs pass (PK-20b). CR-12's fold reads Cyrillic and Greek look-alikes as Latin, but not Cherokee
  (`ᎪU`, `ᏟᎪNON`) or Latin small capitals (`ᴀᴜ`, `ᴄᴀɴᴏɴ`): written onto a `[BWRK]` page at scope `write`.
- **Fix:** the fold (src/facts/rules.ts) also reads Latin small capitals and the Cherokee and Lisu letters that look
  like Latin letters (both case forms of Cherokee, since lower-casing changes them) as those letters; CR-12's tests stay
  green.
- **Test:** `test/review/r2-packs/tags.test.ts` (PK-20b).

### R2-16 (Critical): Revert compared chronology rows and events by their words only

- **Found by:** the Revert pass (RV-A10 to RV-A13). At the preview and inside the lock a row was compared by its
  description, Date and KCY text, and an event by its name, start, end, detail, lane and uri; the revert then wrote the
  row's old block back whole, or deleted the row and event. An owner's link or bold in a row, an inline icon (lost for
  good: the API cannot write one), or an event's colour, icon or image added after the job was overwritten or deleted,
  and the revert reported success.
- **Fix:** the target records what the job left: each row block it wrote, and each event as LegendKeeper answered the
  job's last write of it. A row is compared whole (normalised as `compareStoredBlocks` does) and an event by every
  stored field of the spec's `Event` (an event with no recorded answer counts as changed), at the preview and inside the
  lock: the table check takes the whole block and `exactEvents` checks absent fields as null, both opt-in, set only by
  Revert (the chronology tools are unchanged; every test in test/chronology passes). Sandbox W16 checks that an event
  reads back as its write answered it.
- **Test:** `test/review/r2-revert/chronology-content.test.ts` (4 tests).

### R2-17 (Critical): a facts revert wrote into another property after a rename

- **Found by:** the Revert pass (RV-A15). The job's property was found and compared by its id, but the revert was
  planned with `set_facts`' planner by the title it had at the job, and that planner takes whatever property carries the

### R2-18 (High): Last Major Revision put back wrongly

### R2-20 (High): "all or nothing" broke inside the revert job

### R2-33 (Medium): a pinned governance section was matched by its first heading only

### R2-39 (High): a snapshot image restore uploaded an agent's re-encoded copy

## 3. What was checked and found sound

So later sessions know what has been reviewed. Each was checked by reading the code and, where noted, by the new tests
that held (section 4).

**Write scope (the engine).** The rails are asked once at entry, after the gate and both guards, only about a request
that would otherwise run (`authorize`, src/jobs/engine.ts): never about approved work, the bridge's own, or a request
already proposed; a rail that throws fails closed. Every LegendKeeper write operation names its page in
`path.resourceId` (the registry's path templates, checked one by one), so the bulk rail sees document, block, row,
event, feature and board writes. The low-level tools carry only `allowUnsupportedLoss` and `confirm` into a step's
options (src/mcp/tools/lk-api.ts); every option a rail or a guard trusts (`journalOperation`, `stagedPlacement`,
`bindFrom`, `readBefore`) is set by bridge code. A direct job is asked again before each step, so a lowered scope or a
revoked connection stops it (WS-G4 held); `operation_id` answers the same at both scopes, a rail's proposal included
(WS-G1, G2 held); the per-operation tools meet the same rails (WS-R1 held); the asset rail catches moves to another
folder, to the root, folder deletes and a move hidden in a batch (WS-F0a to F0d held). CR-08, CR-09, CR-10 and CR-16
were tried again under write scope and held (WS-B4, WS-D1d, the restore control).

**ADR 0055 decision E (a direct `updateBlock` read first).** The caller's If-Match still wins over the read's version
and a mismatch is a `VERSION_CONFLICT` (the compare-and-swap in src/jobs/step-exec.ts); the read is made only when the
Last Major Revision rule needs it.

**Revert.** It runs as the system principal, so both guards refuse it any protected change; page text is compared
strictly at the preview and by a hash inside the lock; duplicate fact keys are refused; the engine's guard keeps
`unsupported` blocks through the revert's block writes and the whole-page fallback (RV-A34 to RV-A37 held); every write
it cannot put back is listed as not revertible (src/review/targets.ts, section 7 of `jobTargets`), so a partial revert
needs the owner's confirmation. The routes need the owner's session, a fresh re-authentication, CSRF and `confirm: true`
(RV-A27 to RV-A29 held), and refuse another project's job, a running job, an approved proposal's job and the bridge's
own (RV-A30 to RV-A33 held); sidebar rows re-ordered since are fine (RV-A26).

**The image paths.** The SSRF guards held completely: every literal address spelling, DNS answers and rebinding, the
signed PUT, the asset-host GETs, redirects, ports, schemes and credentials (IM-A1 to IM-A5). No `download_url`, signed
URL or other secret reached a log line, a table or an answer (IM-D1 to D3). No path traversal worked (files are named by
their hash; ids are checked; IM-C1 to C5). The size caps held against lying lengths, chunked bodies, a slow drip and a
gzip bomb, and the decoder refuses pixel and frame bombs (IM-B2 to B9b). Parallel stagings of one original are
serialised by the conversion queue (IM-E5); four routes into one missing folder chain make one chain (IM-E2).

**The guards and the packs.** A pending pack version cannot switch a package tool (`PACK_NOT_CURRENT`, PK-03); a tool
with no switch proposes (PK-04); an unreadable governance document fails closed (PK-06); `promoteNation` through a
"follow the scope" switch is still a canonical proposal (PK-10); the name and body fixes check the page again (PK-14,
PK-15); a handed-over audit fix stays a proposal (PK-19); AU look-alikes inside the fold, a CANON-for-AU swap and a
pending version dropping the AU ruling all held (PK-20a, PK-21, PK-22); the pack admin routes need the owner and a fresh
re-authentication (PK-24). **Appraisal writes nothing:** every appraisal path writes only the bridge's own index tables
(src/appraisal/overlay.ts, fts.ts), the four reports wrote and proposed nothing against the mock (PK-12, PK-27), and
`arkona_appraisal_fix` is refused at scope `read` (PK-26); its one route to LegendKeeper is a job through the engine and
the gate.

**Snapshots and restores.** A restore is a proposal at every scope and compare-and-swap at execution (the blockwise
check, If-Match, the map and timeline checks); a full snapshot that could not take a piece refuses it
(`SNAPSHOT_PIECE_MISSING`) rather than restoring an older one; a change snapshot falls back to the newest earlier piece
by design (`stateAt`). A recreated page never takes a status tag (only the lifecycle tools add them).

**Chronology.** Every `listEvents` reader but one follows the cursor (the one: R2-47). The orphan delete's assertion
inside the lock (`row.orphan`) reads the table again; a table-only row takes no part in pairing; rows shifted by a later
event are caught by the count check (RV-A14).

**Last Major Revision.** Its steps never fail the job (`followUp: 'lmr_failed'`); the read before a direct `updateBlock`
is made only when the page has the row and the mode is `auto`; the rails count no extra page for it. Nothing found
crashes or fails a write because of it; what it gets wrong on Revert is R2-18.

**Auth and the dashboard.** No change to OAuth, sessions or the CSRF guard since `08feec9`. Every route added since is
under `/api/admin` (the owner's session, Origin, the CSRF token, `Sec-Fetch-Site`); the Google Drive callback outside it
needs an owner session and a pending sign-in of that session; request bodies are parsed with zod (two loose objects are
validated further down: the audit configuration, the lifecycle patch); the public pages escape the contact email.
Re-authentication is asked for the rails, Revert, pack approval, remap, tool switches and token maps, Make default, the
canon rules, the lifecycle settings, the audit configuration, the off-site copy, project keys and removal, the staging
"allow delete" and the protecting maintenance jobs (and now the scope raises: R2-50, R2-51).

**The governor.** Learned tiers are kept by group and limit, never attributed to a documented tier for a listed group,
and expire after two windows unseen; the measured write duration is the median of the last 200 write steps (negative
durations left out), floored at the configured spacing; an estimate never stops a restore being planned.

## 8. The sandbox run (for the owner)

```bash
DB_PATH=… LKB_ENV_FILE=/path/to/lk-bridge.env LKB_SANDBOX_PROJECT=test npm run test:sandbox -- assumptions-r2
```

On the server: `sudo -u lkbridge /opt/lk-bridge/app/deploy/sandbox-test.sh --project test -- assumptions-r2`. It runs
round 2 alone (`-- assumptions.test` runs round 1 alone; `-- assumptions` runs both). It refuses to run unless `test` is
a registered sandbox project and the key's own project is exactly that one, and it makes every call through the bridge's
engine and governor. It creates one throwaway root (`lk-bridge assumptions-r2 <time>`) with nine plainly named pages
under it, one template (`lk-bridge assumptions-r2 template W13`, outside the root, as templates are), four asset folders
and one 70-byte PNG; it deletes all of it at the end, even after a failure, and names any id it could not delete. Cost:
52 writes and 121 reads, 3 downloads from the asset host and 1 PUT to the upload host; about a minute at the bridge's
write pacing. It prints PASS, FAIL, INCONCLUSIVE or NOTED per assumption with what LegendKeeper answered, and fails if
any assumption FAILs. Send the report back: each FAIL is a place where the bridge relies on LegendKeeper behaving
otherwise than it does. Round 1 (`-- assumptions.test`) is worth running again too: its A10 changed (section 6), and
R2-10, R2-19 and R2-18 rely on A10, A6 and B16.
