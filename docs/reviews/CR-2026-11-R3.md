# CR-2026-11-R3: the third independent review (splits, renames, retagging, selective approval and Resume)

**Critical, fixed in this branch: on the reviewed code an agent at scope `write` could get canon text the owner had
left out of an approved rename rewritten** (R3-12, R3-25). An ordinary direct edit of the same page while the rename
waited for approval or ran (any writer's, an agent's at scope `write` included) made the rename's rebase apply the edits
of items the owner had unticked, quotations and history among them; the job reported them verified, and Revert does not
apply to an approved rename. Three more critical findings need the owner's own approval or Revert: an approved split
lost a moved block with no text while reporting it verified (R3-18), and a confirmed partial Revert of a split left the
moved sections on no live page, then proposed deleting their only copy as "back in the source" (R3-09, R3-10). All are
fixed here with every other critical and high finding; deploy this before the next owner-approved rename or split. So are
the owner's three later reports: memory retained in normal operation on the live bridge (R3-65), and two split defects
found on the test world (R3-66: a Compact Character Reference left at the head of several owners; R3-67: a split
reported "verified" while its job's verification said otherwise).

The third independent review, from `main` at `6eeff4c` (F3f, #72): everything merged after the second review
(`88c640e`, #67) and F3.5 (`3f10c71`, #66): the block-id sweep (ADR 0056), R2.1's decisions (ADR 0057, #68), F3d's
renames, selective approval and Resume (ADR 0058, #69) and F3d.1 (#70), F3e's splits (ADR 0059, #71) and F3f's
retagging (ADR 0060, #72). With them, the owner's three requests: a clean shutdown on SIGTERM (A), the memory of the
largest jobs (B) and an honest retag read-back (C), in section 5.

**How it was done.** As CR-2026-11 and CR-2026-11-R2 (their method and format). The reviewer did not write the code and
treated every "verified", "tested" and "covered" in the ADRs, the changelog and the code as a claim to check. Four
separate reviewers, each in an isolated copy of the repository and each given the code and the ADRs rather than the
lead's conclusions, tried to: (1) lose content with a split reported verified; (2) make a rename edit text it should not,
or leave a half-renamed identity unreported; (3) make retagging add or remove a status or lifecycle tag, or apply a
hold; (4) make selective approval or Resume run something the owner did not approve. Section 4 lists every attempt,
successful or not (208). The lead reviewer read the block-id sweep, the R2.1 decisions, the engine's rebase and item
filter, Resume and Revert, and built the owner's fixes; building fix B found two more (R3-01, R3-02). Every finding below
was reproduced by a test that asserts the safe behaviour and **fails on the reviewed code**; each fix makes it pass.
**Nothing was run against a live system**: every test runs against the mock LegendKeeper in this repository; the
owner's LegendKeeper bridge connector, the live dashboard and LegendKeeper were not called; no sandbox, soak, spike,
rehearsal or round-trip script was run. The new sandbox checks (section 6) are written and not run: the owner runs them.

**Severity**, as CR-2026-11 defines it: critical — could lose or corrupt data, or pass canon or lifecycle approval; high
— a wrong result or a misleading state; medium — cost or robustness; low — clarity.

### Splits

### R3-09 (Critical): a confirmed partial Revert of a split left the moved sections on no live page

- **What:** Revert lets the owner confirm a partial revert target by target (ADR 0055 B). A split's source edit and its
  destination writes are two halves of one move. After phase 1, one line added to the surviving source made its text
  target "changed since"; the owner confirmed the rest, and the revert job took the appended blocks off the Sun Rite
  while the source could not take them back: `sh4` and `sf1`–`sf6` were on no live page (only the journal and the
  snapshots held them). Once phase 2 has archived the source its archive note changes the source's text, so every Revert
  of phase 1 was partial, and lost every moved section (SP-03).
- **Reproduced by:** `test/review/r3-split/revert.test.ts` SP-01, SP-03.
- **Fixed:** a split's phase 1 is reverted whole or not at all: `SPLIT_REVERT_NOT_WHOLE` names the targets that cannot go
  back and what to do (a source phase 2 archived is unarchived first) (`src/review/revert.ts`); the preview says
  `split.wholeOnly` and the Review page offers no partial revert for a split (`ui/src/pages/review.tsx`). ADR 0061 A1.
- **Changed test:** `test/packs/split.test.ts` (the preview's `split` now carries `wholeOnly: true`).

### R3-10 (Critical): the deletion proposal after a Revert said the material was back, and deleted its only copy

- **What:** after any successful revert job, the split proposed deleting the pages it made, saying "the moved material is
  back in <source>" whether it was or not; after R3-09's partial revert, approving it deleted the only live copy of
  `sh3` and `sl1`–`sl6`.
- **Reproduced by:** `test/review/r3-split/revert.test.ts` SP-02, and SP-02b (the follow-up called directly with the
  material not back).
- **Fixed:** a page the split made is proposed for deletion only once each block it took is read back in the source,
  under its id and by its whole content; otherwise it is kept and listed with why (`src/packs/split/service.ts`
  `notBackInSource`).

### R3-18 (Critical): a moved block with no text vanished, and the split reported it verified

- **What:** the Faith section's key-value block `sfkv` was appended to the Sun Rite, which already had a divider whose id
  was `sfkv`. The append saw the id and treated itself as done; both checks looked up the original id first and compared
  words only (a divider and a key-value block both have none); the source edit then removed `sfkv`. Its rows were on no
  page; the job succeeded, verified, and phase 2 was proposed. Block ids are unique only within a document, and splits
  copy ids between pages, so the clash is realistic. The same lookup made a split whose destination already used a
  moved block's id (with other text) impossible to finish (SP-14, medium, ADR 0059 departure 2).
- **Reproduced by:** `test/review/r3-split/content.test.ts` SP-15, SP-14.
- **Fixed:** where a moved block landed is found by its whole content (`contentPrint`: every field and child, ids and
  versions aside, normalised as LegendKeeper stores runs), under its own id or the derived one; a page's own block that
  shares the id is never taken for it, in the append, the destinations check and the verification
  (`src/packs/split/blocks.ts` `findMoved`, `landedIds`, `applyAppend`, `contentProblems`; `src/packs/split/checks.ts`).

### R3-17 (High): "moved verbatim" was checked by the block's words, ignoring case

- **What:** modelled (ADR 0059 departure 10 admits LegendKeeper may not store a block as sent): a heading level 2 → 4,
  a statute lower-cased, a table cell's italic and link dropped, header flags removed, an expand's title replaced,
  key-value rows emptied, a code block's language changed, a nested list moved: both checks passed and the originals
  were removed from the source (`textPrint`, `blockText`, `textKey` lower-casing).
- **Reproduced by:** `test/review/r3-split/content.test.ts` SP-13.
- **Fixed:** the plan records each original block's content print; the destinations check and the verification compare
  it (`src/packs/split/plan.ts`, `checks.ts`; `normaliseForCompare` in `src/jobs/verify-blocks.ts`).

### R3-21 (High): the deletion after a Revert was not checked again when it ran, and "unchanged" meant "same words"

- **What:** "only while it is exactly as the split made it" was checked when the proposal was made: a paragraph added to
  the Law page while the proposal waited was deleted with it (SP-04); a page whose statute the owner made bold and linked
  was still proposed (SP-05).
- **Reproduced by:** `test/review/r3-split/revert.test.ts` SP-04, SP-05.
- **Fixed:** each page is compared by content print when proposed, and checked again just before its deletion inside the
  approved job (`split.made`, refusal `SPLIT_PAGE_CHANGED`) (`src/packs/split/checks.ts`, `service.ts`).

### R3-19 (High): a section deleted, or moved by hand, after the plan was made again from the plan's copy

## 5. The owner's fixes

### A. A clean shutdown on SIGTERM, within the stop timeout

On 18 November a deploy's SIGTERM did not end the bridge within systemd's 45-second stop timeout while a maintenance job
ran, and systemd killed it. **Built:**

- **New work refused.** From the first moment of the stop, every request answers 503 with "The bridge is restarting; send
  this again in a minute" (`BRIDGE_RESTARTING`: MCP as a JSON-RPC error, the dashboard's API as an error body, `/healthz`
  as `{ ok: false, restarting: true }`) (`src/server.ts`).
- **Requests in flight finish.** A request already sent to LegendKeeper is let finish, for at most 10 s; nothing is sent
  after it (`src/lk/governor/governor.ts` `stopWaits`; `src/projects/registry.ts`).
- **Jobs stop at a step boundary, never part-way through a write**, recorded queued with `waitReason.restart` ("stopped at
  a step boundary by a restart; it continues when the bridge starts") and the step they continue from, and continue by
  themselves when the bridge starts (`src/jobs/engine.ts` `stopForRestart`, `parkForRestart`).
- **Waits end at once**: a budget or pacing wait, a lock or slot wait and a retry's back-off; a job parked for budget
  keeps its time (`RestartWaitError`, `src/lk/errors.ts`; the engine's combined abort signals). The maintenance
  scheduler and the block-id sweep stop between documents.
- **Then** the HTTP server and the database close and the process exits; a 25-second watchdog exits whatever is left.
  `TimeoutStopSec=75` in `deploy/lk-bridge.service` is the cushion (`docs/runbook-deploy.md`).
- **Measured** (the tests stop the real bridge exactly as SIGTERM does, then start a new one on the same database and
  mock): 1,526 ms with a write in flight for 1.5 s (it waits for that write and nothing else); 22 ms during a retry's 3-second back-off after a 429; 26 ms with a job waiting for the owner's approval of a pack version; 4,043 ms with the block-id sweep's `assignBlockIds` in flight for 4 s; and, asserted, under 2 s with a job parked for budget and under 5 s with a job waiting for a page another job holds. On the reviewed code the same stop with a write in flight took 20,211 ms, most of it waiting.
- **Tests:** `test/review/r3-shutdown/restart.test.ts` (a long job mid-run: the write in flight finishes, the job stops
  at the next boundary and finishes after the restart with nothing sent twice; a retry's back-off; a job parked for
  budget resuming when due; a lock wait; the block-id sweep running), `restart-pack-version.test.ts` (a job waiting for
  the owner's approval of a pack version keeps waiting, writes nothing, and runs once the version is approved), and
  `test/unit/governor-restart.test.ts`.

### C. An honest retag read-back

The read-back said "documents unchanged" from getResource's per-document metadata, and LegendKeeper's spec makes
`Document.updatedAt` optional (the mock omits it), so a body edited during the job was reported "written and read back
exactly" (RT-80). **Built:** the check reads each page document's version before and after the write
(`getDocumentBlocks`, one read a document, counted in the tool's cost) and compares it. A page that verifies now says:
"written and read back: the tags exactly as proposed; name, aliases, parentage and sidebar unchanged; each page
document's body at the same version as before the write; other documents (board, map, timeline) compared by their
metadata timestamps only, not their content". A difference names what moved: "document <id> body unchanged (by its
version)" (a body that could not be read on either side is reported, never taken as unchanged), or "documents' metadata
(id, name, type, place, flags, metadata timestamp) unchanged". ADR 0060 departure 3 is amended; `docs/tools.md` and
`docs/bridge-guide.md` say so. Tests: `test/review/r3-fixes/retag-readback.test.ts`, RT-80 and RT-81.
